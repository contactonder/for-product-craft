Everyone is talking about the Anthropic IPO, and the headline numbers are easy to repeat.

Net revenue retention (NRR, which compares what the same customers spend now with what they spent a year ago) is about 900%. Revenue per employee is above $6.6 million, up from $1.4 million a year earlier. In the first half of 2026, compute (the computing power needed to train and run AI models) cost about 55 cents for every dollar of revenue. A year earlier, it cost about two dollars.

These numbers measure growth and efficiency. They do not answer a different question, the one product leaders should ask about any revenue line: who is paying, and will that revenue stay?

## Read the Headline With Its Definition

The filing defines NRR carefully. It looks at customers who spent at least one dollar in a quarter a year ago, and compares that quarter with the same quarter today. It leaves out the fixed price Pro and Max subscribers.

This is a fair way to measure expansion. But a customer who spent one dollar counts, so the base includes very small accounts. The number tells you how fast a wide base grows its usage. It does not tell you how a typical large customer behaves. Most of the revenue is also paid by usage, so revenue rises whenever customers use more.

That is the first lesson. A metric is a growth story and a definition together. Read both.

## Concentration Has Three Layers

Most people hear "concentration risk" and think of size: one customer is too big. That is one layer. The filing shows three.

The first two layers are about who pays and how the money travels. The third is about why they pay. It is the least familiar, so it deserves the most attention.

## Revenue That Is Not Expected to Repeat

In the filing, Anthropic says its revenue includes money from customers it later found were breaking its rules. It adds that this revenue would not be expected to come back once their access is restricted or ended.

The company also says it strengthened detection and enforcement in August and September 2026. It expects the share to fall, and it warns that this could reduce revenue and growth rates in future periods. It also admits that detection takes time and that customers can try to return under new accounts.

So this is not a stable base. It is revenue the company itself plans to reduce.

## What About Distillation?

Distillation means using one model's outputs to train another model. The filing names it as a competitive risk. Outside reports say some of the rule breaking involves what Anthropic believes is unauthorized distillation.

But we should be careful. The filing does not name the customers, and it does not say they are all competitors. The category of violations is wider than distillation. A fair reading is that some of this revenue may come from current or future rivals. That is a reasonable concern. It is not a disclosed fact.

## What Product Leaders Can Take From This

You do not need to run a frontier AI lab to learn from this. Three questions apply to almost any product:

1. **Who is really paying?** Know the size, the channel, and the intent behind each large revenue line.
2. **Would you keep this revenue if you understood it fully?** Some customers are using your product to build a rival, to resell it, or to avoid your rules. Growth that you would cancel on inspection is not quality growth.
3. **Can you detect it early?** Detection is a product capability, not only a legal task. It needs usage signals, account checks, and a clear policy that you enforce.

This connects to an idea from my earlier posts. A product that sells intelligence, or any strong capability, teaches its customers as it serves them. Some customers will try to learn more than the contract allows.

## The Open Question

My own view is that where the prize is this large, some buyers will test every limit. Rules matter, and so does the ability to notice when they are broken. The filing also says Anthropic believes this is common across the industry.

Will this lead to one lasting winner? The filing points to one honest answer. More than 80% of third quarter revenue came from model versions released in the previous six months. In this market, a lead is not kept. It is earned again every six months.
